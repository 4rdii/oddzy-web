The usual path onto a prediction market involves an exchange account, a browser wallet, a seed phrase, and a bridge. This one doesn't.

## 1. How do you sign up?

Signing up is opening the bot. Start [@poly_sport_bet_bot](https://t.me/poly_sport_bet_bot) in Telegram and tap **Start**. That's the whole signup — no email, no exchange account, nothing to install.

If you'd rather see the whole mechanism before you touch it, [how it works](/how-it-works) is the one-page version.

## 2. Where does your wallet come from?

Opening the mini app generates a self-custodial wallet through Privy, tied to your Telegram login. There's no seed phrase to write down and no extension to install.

**Self-custodial** is the important word: the keys are yours. Oddzy can sign orders you approve, and you can export the private key or revoke that permission at any time. Your balance is never held by us — [where your money actually sits](/learn/is-it-safe-where-your-money-sits) goes through that in detail.

> If you're on Telegram Desktop for Linux, use your phone or a browser for wallet setup — the desktop webview has a known issue initialising the wallet.

## 3. How do you fund the account?

The **Deposit** button asks which network you're sending from, then gives you the address for that network. Deposits are picked up automatically and your balance updates in the bot. A small first deposit is fine: the minimum stake is $1.

- **Always use the address the Deposit button just gave you.** The two fixed addresses on the wallet screen are there so you can look your account up on-chain — never send funds to them.
- Sending from an exchange? Double-check the network at withdrawal. Anything sent on a network the address doesn't cover won't arrive.

## 4. How do you find a market worth trading?

Browse by category, or open the mini app for the full feed sorted by 24-hour volume. Each market shows the YES and NO price in cents, the volume traded, and when it resolves.

Start where you actually know something. An informational edge is the only durable one — and two things are worth reading before your first position: [how a price gets set](/learn/what-is-a-prediction-market), because the price is a probability, and [the resolution rules](/learn/read-the-resolution-rules-first), because they decide what counts as YES.

## 5. What happens when you place a position?

Pick a side, choose a stake, and check the payout preview before confirming. Confirm, and the order is signed and submitted on-chain — you get a receipt with the transaction hash.

The preview holds three numbers:

- **Price** — what one share costs, in cents.
- **Shares** — how many you get for your stake.
- **Payout if correct** — what those shares pay at $1 each.

The price is the whole arithmetic. A $10 stake buys very different things depending on what the market thinks:

| Price you pay | Shares for $10 | Payout if correct | Profit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 25¢ | 40 | $40.00 | $30.00 |
| 40¢ | 25 | $25.00 | $15.00 |
| 65¢ | 15.38 | $15.38 | $5.38 |

The price you get can be worse than the one on screen if the market is thin — [why an order fills at a worse price](/learn/why-did-my-order-fill-at-a-worse-price) explains when that happens and how to avoid the worst of it.

## 6. Can you get out before the market resolves?

Yes. Your position appears under **Positions** with live P&L, and you can exit at the current market price whenever your view changes rather than waiting for the outcome. Holding to resolution is the other option: when the market resolves, winning shares pay $1 each straight to your wallet.

Exiting early is not free — you sell at whatever the market is paying now, which may be less than you paid. [Selling before a market resolves](/learn/can-you-sell-before-a-market-resolves) covers when it's worth it.

## What can go wrong?

You can lose your entire stake. Prices move against you, thin markets can fill worse than the quote suggests, and a market you were "sure" about resolves the other way often enough to matter. Stake accordingly. 18+.
