F1 prediction markets turn a Grand Prix into a set of yes-or-no questions you can trade. Will Max Verstappen win in Las Vegas. Will Lando Norris finish on the podium. Each share pays $1 if the answer is yes and $0 if not, so the price in cents is the probability the market puts on it.

Formula 1 has a problem most sports do not. The order in which cars cross the line is not always the result. Time penalties are added afterwards, and a car can be disqualified in scrutineering hours later. The markets handle this by naming one document and ignoring what happens after it.

## What is an F1 prediction market?

It is one question about one session of a race weekend, with its own order book. A driver’s winner market, podium market and pole market are separate markets with separate traders and separate rules. As of 20 November 2026 on Oddzy there were 712 active Formula 1 markets: 70 race winner markets, 599 race props and 43 season markets.

The Las Vegas Grand Prix is scheduled for 21 November 2026. At 07:12 UTC on 20 November the winner markets looked like this:

| Las Vegas GP winner | Card price | Cheapest to buy | 24h volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Max Verstappen | 39¢ | 40¢ | $408 |
| George Russell | 17¢ | 18¢ | $2,005 |
| Kimi Antonelli | 16¢ | 18¢ | $3,966 |
| Lando Norris | 14¢ | 15¢ | $344 |
| Lewis Hamilton | 5¢ | 6¢ | $99 |
| Charles Leclerc | 4.5¢ | 5¢ | $89 |

All 23 driver markets together traded $7,317 in 24 hours. The pole markets traded $1,704, the podium markets $205 and the safety car market $186. For scale, the busiest market on Oddzy that day, on the Fed’s December decision, traded $1,306,265. F1 books are thin, and the props are thinner: Antonelli to finish on the podium showed 53¢ on the card and cost 64¢ to buy.

## Does a disqualification after the race change the result?

Only if it arrives before the FIA publishes the Final Classification. Winner, podium and head-to-head markets resolve on that document, which the rules describe as typically released 30–60 minutes after the race and including any time penalties applied. The rules then say that disqualifications or changes made after its publication will not affect resolution. The podium ceremony does not count either.

Last year’s Las Vegas race shows why the timing matters. On 22 November 2025 Lando Norris finished second and Oscar Piastri fourth. Just over three hours after the finish, both McLarens were disqualified for excessive plank wear, as Formula 1 reported at the time.

| Driver | Initial classification | After the disqualifications |
|---|---|---|
| Max Verstappen | 1st | 1st |
| Lando Norris | 2nd | Disqualified |
| George Russell | 3rd | 2nd |
| Oscar Piastri | 4th | Disqualified |
| Kimi Antonelli | 5th, after a 5-second penalty | 3rd |

The winner market is the same in both columns. The podium markets are not: the first column pays Norris and the second pays Antonelli. Which column a market uses depends on when the FIA publishes the Final Classification on the night, not on the 30–60 minute estimate in the rules. You cannot know that when you buy. If a car is under investigation after the flag, treat the market as undecided until it resolves, and [read the resolution rules before the price](/learn/read-the-resolution-rules-first).

## Does a grid penalty change a pole position market?

No. A pole market pays the driver Formula 1 recognises as having set the fastest time in qualifying, regardless of any later penalty, disqualification or change to the starting grid. A driver who qualifies fastest and then drops five places for an engine change still resolves Yes. The question is who was fastest on the day, not who starts first.

## How do head-to-head, fastest lap and safety car markets settle?

Each has one clause that decides the awkward cases.

| Market | Settles on | The clause to know |
|---|---|---|
| Head-to-head | Which driver finishes ahead in the Final Classification | A driver who retires is ranked by laps completed; the same lap count resolves 50-50 |
| Fastest lap | The driver credited with the fastest lap of the race | Practice and qualifying laps never count |
| Safety car | Whether the safety car is deployed during the race | A Virtual Safety Car does not count |
| Constructor score | The team that scores the most points in the Grand Prix | A tie goes to the team whose name comes first alphabetically |

The head-to-head rule means a crash on lap 40 beats a crash on lap 12, even though neither driver is classified. If both stop on the same lap, each share pays 50¢, whichever car stopped first.

## What happens if a Grand Prix is cancelled or postponed?

It depends on the market type, and the rules disagree with each other. The Las Vegas markets give the race until 28 November 2026 to be run.

| Market | Cancelled, or run after the cut-off |
|---|---|
| Winner, pole, fastest lap | Resolves to “Other”, so no named driver is paid |
| Safety car | Resolves 50-50 |
| Podium, head-to-head | Stays open through a postponement; 50-50 only if permanently cancelled |

A winner share bought at 40¢ is worth nothing if the race is called off, while a podium share bought at 80¢ pays 50¢. This is not a remote case this season. As of 20 November 2026 on Oddzy, the market on whether the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix takes place showed 72.5¢. It has traded $13,451 in total, more than any single Las Vegas winner market. The Abu Dhabi winner markets resolve to “Other” if the race is cancelled or moved past 13 December.
