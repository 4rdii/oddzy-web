Tennis prediction markets let you trade a yes-or-no question about a match instead of placing a bet with a bookmaker. Will Carlos Alcaraz beat Frances Tiafoe. Will the match go to a third set. Each share pays $1 if the answer is yes and $0 if not, so the price in cents is the probability the market puts on it.

Tennis has a problem football and the UFC mostly do not. Players retire mid-match, often injured, and players withdraw before a match starts. Each market type on the same match has its own rule for both, and the rules do not agree.

## What is a tennis prediction market?

It is one question about one match, with its own order book. The winner market pays whichever player the ATP or WTA records as advancing. The set handicap, game handicap, total games, total sets and set-winner markets are separate markets with separate traders and separate settlement rules.

As of 16 November 2026, 07:13 UTC, on Oddzy, there were 1,032 active ATP match markets and 159 WTA ones. The deepest was the ATP Finals round-robin match between Alcaraz and Tiafoe in Turin:

| Market (Alcaraz vs Tiafoe) | Card price | Cheapest to buy | 24h volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winner, Alcaraz / Tiafoe | 88.5¢ / 11.5¢ | 89¢ / 12¢ | $97,763 |
| Set handicap, Alcaraz −1.5 | 64.5¢ | 65¢ | $1,567 |
| Total games, Over 22.5 | 40.5¢ | 41¢ | $1,501 |
| Total sets, Over 2.5 | 31¢ | 32¢ | $264 |
| Set 2 winner, Alcaraz | 81.5¢ | 83¢ | $45 |

The winner market had traded $98,150 in total, so nearly all of it arrived in the final 24 hours. The two set markets also check each other. Under 2.5 sets at 69¢ is a straight-sets win for either player, and Alcaraz in straight sets is 64.5¢, which leaves roughly 4.5¢ for Tiafoe winning 2–0.

## How does a tennis market settle a retirement?

The winner market pays in full; the others usually split. Once a match has started, a retirement, default or disqualification resolves the winner market to the player who advances. The set handicap, game handicap and total markets resolve 50-50, every share paying 50¢, unless the score at the moment play stopped had already decided them.

"Already decided" has a strict meaning in the rules. An outcome counts only if it would have happened under every possible way the match could have finished, under the format the tournament published for that round. Two retirements, both by Tiafoe, show the difference, assuming a full third set:

| Market | Retires at 6–3, 2–1 | Retires at 6–3, 3–6, 1–0 |
|---|---|---|
| Winner | Alcaraz | Alcaraz |
| Set handicap, Alcaraz −1.5 | 50-50 | Tiafoe (+1.5) |
| Total games, 22.5 | 50-50 | Over |
| Total sets, 2.5 | 50-50 | Over |
| Set 1 winner | Alcaraz | Alcaraz |
| Set 2 winner | 50-50 | Tiafoe |

In the first case, 12 games have been played and the match could still end under 22.5 or go well over it, so the totals split. In the second, a third set has begun, which settles Over 2.5 sets because a set counts as played once it starts. Even a 6–0 third set would take the match to 24 games, so Over 22.5 is certain too. This is the clause that makes it worth [reading the resolution rules before the price](/learn/read-the-resolution-rules-first).

## What happens if a player withdraws before the match starts?

A walkover splits the winner market 50-50. The rules treat a player withdrawing before the first point the same way they treat a cancellation: every share pays 50¢, and the set and game markets do the same.

That is worth a great deal to whoever holds the underdog. Bought at the prices above, an Alcaraz share costs 89¢ and a Tiafoe share 12¢:

| What happens | Alcaraz share (cost 89¢) | Tiafoe share (cost 12¢) |
|---|---|---|
| Alcaraz wins, or Tiafoe retires | +11¢ | −12¢ |
| Walkover, either player | −39¢ | +38¢ |
| Tiafoe wins, or Alcaraz retires | −89¢ | +88¢ |

At the ATP Finals, a player who withdraws mid-event is replaced by an alternate. As written, a match against the alternate is not the match the market names, so the named match was never played and resolves 50-50.

## When does a delayed match resolve 50-50?

Only after a cut-off, typically 14 days after the scheduled start. The total-games and total-sets markets say outright that a postponement or suspension does not trigger a 50-50 resolution; they stay open until the match finishes. For the Alcaraz–Tiafoe markets, scheduled for 16 November 2026, the cut-off is 11:59 PM ET on 30 November. Your money stays in the position until then, and [when you actually get paid](/learn/how-markets-resolve-and-when-you-get-paid) depends on settlement after that.

## Why does the price I pay jump during a match?

Tennis markets trade while the match is played, and the book thins on whichever side looks lost. The Alcaraz–Tiafoe markets list a close time of 00:00 UTC on 23 November, a week after the match. A break of serve can move the card several cents in seconds, and the offer you click may be gone by the time the order arrives. That is [why an order fills at a worse price than the one you saw](/learn/why-did-my-order-fill-at-a-worse-price), and it gets worse on a set market trading $45 a day.

## Before you take a position

Put the walkover and retirement cases into the price before you buy a heavy favourite, because a 50-50 settlement costs a favourite far more than it costs the underdog. Size the stake so you can lose all of it; [how to size a position](/learn/how-to-size-a-position) covers that. The current ATP and WTA match boards are on [the tennis topic page](/topic/tennis).
