## What is this position really betting on?

Not a peace process. Read components three and five: they pay when there is **no** meeting and **no** deal. The basket's own description is honest about it — calm without a deal.

So the thesis is frozen conflict. The shooting stops, the blockade lifts, oil comes back down, and nothing diplomatic happens. If your view is that talks are about to restart, two of these five components are working against you, and you want the [meeting binary at 22.5c](/market/us-x-iran-diplomatic-meeting-by-september-30-2026) instead.

That design also does something useful: it gives the position a floor. Because two components pay when diplomacy fails, a renewed US strike does not wipe you out.

## What has to happen for this to pay?

Treating the prices as independent — which understates the clustered outcomes, since these events move together — the shape looks like this:

| Scenario | Returns | Rough odds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US strikes Iran again | $46.62 | ~17% |
| **Ceasefire holds, nothing else moves** | **$76.56** | **~25%** |
| Plus oil dips to $70 | $123.44 | ~12% |
| Plus blockade announced | $164.28 | ~10% |
| All five hit | $211.16 | ~5% |

The row that matters is the second one. **The single most likely outcome loses you 23%** — the ceasefire holds, there is no meeting, there is no deal, and neither the blockade nor oil moves. That is the status quo simply continuing, and it is the most probable thing that happens.

Which tells you where the position really lives.

## Which component carries the position?

The blockade and oil components are **40% of your stake and 64% of the maximum payout**. Everything else is ballast that returns about three-quarters of your money in the base case.

Narrow it further: the blockade component alone is 25% of the stake and **42% of the upside**, and it is the difference between $76.56 and $164.28. There is no realistic scenario where this basket clears $100 without it.

So the honest description of this position is: a bet that the United States announces the end of the blockade before 30 September, wrapped in four contracts that soften the downside. Whether that wrapper is worth 75% of your stake is the actual decision. Buying [the blockade contract alone at 28.5c](/market/us-announces-end-of-iranian-blockade-by-september-30-2026-20260727171615364-722-649-646-561-213-644) is cleaner and pays more — and gives up the floor.

## Does the ceasefire component protect the others?

No, and this is the trap in holding it as a hedge.

The ceasefire contract resolves **No** only if the United States lands an air strike or a surface-to-surface missile strike that directly impacts Iran. Its text explicitly excludes naval gunfire and artillery, ground incursions, cyber operations, small-arms fire, intercepted munitions, and any threat or authorisation of force that is not carried out. It defines Iran as terrestrial territory and internal waters — **airspace and maritime territory are outside the market**.

The United States can therefore enforce the naval blockade with gunfire against Iranian shipping in Iranian waters, and this contract still pays **Yes** while your blockade component goes to zero. The two are not opposite sides of anything. This is why you [read the resolution rules before the price](/learn/read-the-resolution-rules-first).

## Which component is priced better than its twin?

The no-meeting component, and it is a genuine point in the basket's favour.

The standalone meeting binary trades at 22.5c, which puts its No side at **77.5c**. The basket instead owns *no qualifying meeting* at **63.9c**. Both contracts use the same qualifying language for what counts as a meeting, so that is **13.6 cents cheaper for the same outcome**.

The reason the gap survives is structural: the 63.9c contract is the "none" bucket of a categorical market on which country hosts the next round, and arbitraging it would mean trading every venue bucket at once. Worth knowing why the meeting is unlikely at all — the market's text notes the first round concluded in Switzerland on 22 June, then rules that follow-on technical talks from that round **do not qualify by themselves**. It needs a fresh senior-level round.

## When do you find out you are wrong?

Direction is not what you are buying. The market already gives the blockade announcement **70.55%** by year end. The September contract at 28.5c prices *when*. You are betting on speed, and the shorter-dated contracts on the same question are public checkpoints:

| 31 Aug | 7 Sep | 14 Sep | 21 Sep | 30 Sep | 31 Oct | 31 Dec |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.80c | 9.50c | 20.5c | 23.5c | **28.5c** | 50.5c | 70.55c |

If nothing has happened by 7 September, about 9 of your 28 points are gone with three weeks left. By 21 September the ladder has only 5 points of room above where the month started. You do not have to wait for the deadline — mark the position against these rungs and [sell the components you no longer believe in](/learn/can-you-sell-before-a-market-resolves) while they still carry value.

The ceasefire ladder runs the other way: 96.15c at 31 August, 89.5c at 15 September, 83.5c at 30 September. That decay is the market pricing a **12.7% chance the US strikes Iranian soil during September**.

## What might not fill, and how big should it be?

The oil component is the problem. It has **$502 of lifetime volume** and $263 in the last 24 hours — it is not tradeable in size, and it is not indexable on Oddzy for that reason. At a 15% weight, any order above a few hundred dollars will likely leave that component unfilled, and [that portion returns to your balance](/learn/what-is-a-basket) rather than moving into the others. Plan for this as a four-component position at size.

For sizing, remember that three of the five components resolve off the same underlying question, so this is closer to one position than five. [Size it accordingly](/learn/how-to-size-a-position). The smallest weight also sets the minimum for the whole order, so the 15% component determines how small you can go.
