The [Iran de-escalation basket](/basket/iran-de-escalation) holds five contracts, all settling by 30 September. Three of them are already close to decided. Two of them carry the entire trade. This is what each scenario returns and which prices to check first.

All figures are from Oddzy's market data as of 27 August 2026. Prices move daily — check the basket page before you buy.

**$100 in · $224.40 back if all five hit · blended probability 63.7%.**

Bought as single shares instead, the five cost **$3.185 for a maximum of $5** — a 57% return.

## What is the market actually pricing?

Read the column of prices from the top. Both ceasefires holding and the airspace staying open sit between 83.5c and 94.5c. A new round of talks sits at 22.5c.

That is the whole story of the Iran board in one line: **the market has priced calm, and it has not priced reconciliation.** The guns being quiet is near-consensus. The two sides sitting back down is a one-in-five shot.

The basket buys both. Which means most of what you pay goes on the part that is already settled.

## Which contracts are you actually paying for?

Split the five by how decided they are.

The three calm contracts — 88.5c, 94.5c and 83.5c — sum to **266.5c of the 318.5c cost, or 84%**. Bought alone as shares they return a maximum of $3.00, so their profit is **33.5 cents**. You are handing over most of the money to be told something the price has already told you.

The two live contracts — the blockade at 29.5c and talks at 22.5c — cost **52 cents** and return a maximum of $2.00.

| Structure | Cost per share | Maximum | Return |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All five | $3.185 | $5.00 | +57% |
| The three calm contracts | $2.665 | $3.00 | +12.6% |
| **Blockade and talks only** | **$0.52** | **$2.00** | **+285%** |

The same imbalance shows up in the weighted basket: those two contracts are 40% of the stake and about **70% of the maximum payout**, because a 20-dollar allocation buys far more shares at 22.5c than at 94.5c.

## What does each scenario return?

Treating the prices as independent — which understates the clustered outcomes, since these events move together — the shape is this:

| Scenario | Returns on $100 | Rough odds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US strikes Iranian soil again | $23–$44 | ~17% |
| **Only the three calm contracts hit** | **$67.71** | **~38%** |
| Plus the blockade is called off | $135.51 | ~16% |
| Plus talks convene | $156.60 | ~11% |
| All five | $224.40 | ~5% |

The second row is the one to sit with. **The single most likely outcome is a 32% loss** — the ceasefires hold, the airspace stays open, and neither the blockade nor the talks move. That is simply the last six weeks continuing, and it is the most probable thing that happens.

Note the top row too. Every contract here pays on calm, so **this basket has no hedge**. There is no leg that wins if fighting resumes.

## Are you betting on direction or on speed?

Direction is close to consensus already. The market gives the blockade announcement **70.55%** by 31 December against 29.5c for September:

| 31 Aug | 7 Sep | 14 Sep | 21 Sep | 30 Sep | 31 Oct | 31 Dec |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.80c | 9.50c | 20.5c | 23.5c | **29.5c** | 50.5c | 70.55c |

The market expects the blockade to end. It does not expect it this month. Buying the September contract is a claim that it happens **faster than the board thinks** — a much harder call than "tension is falling", and the one you are actually making.

The airspace contract runs the other way: full closure is 5.5c by 30 September but 17c by 31 December, so the No side decays from 94.5c to 83c. Your protection there is cheap precisely because the window is short.

## What does the ceasefire contract actually cover?

Less than the name suggests, and it matters because this contract is a fifth of the basket.

It resolves **No** only if the United States lands an air strike or a surface-to-surface missile strike that directly impacts Iran. Its text explicitly excludes naval gunfire and artillery, ground incursions, cyber operations, small-arms fire, intercepted munitions, and any threat or authorisation of force that is not carried out. It defines Iran as terrestrial territory and internal waters — **airspace and maritime territory are outside it**.

So the United States could enforce the naval blockade with gunfire against Iranian shipping in Iranian waters and this contract still pays **Yes**, while your blockade contract goes to zero. Reading these texts is not optional in a basket where two legs sound like the same event and are not — which is the whole point of [reading the resolution rules before the price](/learn/read-the-resolution-rules-first).

## When do you find out you are wrong?

The shorter-dated blockade contracts are public checkpoints. If nothing has happened by 7 September, roughly 9 of your 29 points are gone with three weeks left; by 21 September the ladder sits only 6 points above where the month started. You do not need to wait for the deadline — mark the position against those rungs and [sell what you no longer believe in](/learn/can-you-sell-before-a-market-resolves) while it still carries value.

For the talks contract, the thing to watch is the venue board. Switzerland leads at 8.5c, where the June round was held, then Pakistan at 4.8c and Qatar at 3.55c. A venue firming up is the earliest signal that the cheapest contract in this basket is about to work.

## How big, and what might not fill?

Two contracts are thin. The Israel–Iran ceasefire moved **$4,272** in the last 24 hours and the airspace contract **$7,580**, against $171,699 and $128,051 for the two US–Iran contracts. At a 20% weight they are fine at small size; a large order will leave them partly unfilled, and [that portion returns to your balance](/learn/what-is-a-basket) rather than shifting into the others.

For sizing, treat this as roughly one position rather than five: three of the contracts move together with whether the US holds fire, which the market quotes on its own at 83.5c. [Size it accordingly](/learn/how-to-size-a-position).
