The best Polymarket traders to follow are not the ones with the most profit. They are the ones whose profit is hardest to explain by luck: many resolved bets, returns well above what the prices implied, and few losing weeks. As of 25 November 2026, ranked that way across 2,525 wallets on the [Oddzy trader library](/traders), the leaders are mostly specialists most people have never heard of.

## Why is a profit leaderboard a poor guide to who is good?

A profit leaderboard rewards size and luck as much as judgement. Someone who stakes $1 million on 18 games and is right 10 times can out-earn a careful trader with a thousand bets, and the leaderboard cannot tell the two apart.

That case is real. In the sports list as of 25 November 2026, GetsOnBase shows $737,720 of profit from $1,068,388 risked on just 18 events, with a typical bet of $24,386. It is labelled a whale with few bets. Eighteen results is not enough to separate a sharp from a lucky streak, so it ranks below traders who have earned far less.

## How are the best Polymarket traders ranked by skill?

The ranking compares each trader’s results with what the prices they paid implied. A share bought at 30¢ should win about 30% of the time; skill is how far a trader beats that, measured in standard deviations of luck. The best single event is then removed and the test repeated, so one longshot cannot carry a record.

Around that sit three checks: the share of weeks in profit, how deep the worst losing run went, and whether you could realistically have got the same prices. To be ranked at all, a wallet needs at least 25 resolved events, $5,000 risked and $1,000 of profit in the last 12 months. As of 25 November 2026, 234 of the 2,525 wallets analysed cleared that bar.

## Why do weather traders crowd the top?

Weather markets produce many small, quickly resolved bets, which is exactly what a skill test needs to separate an edge from noise. As of 25 November 2026, 88 wallets qualify in weather, more than in any other category, and four of the top ten trade it mainly.

Read their win rates carefully. HighTempTation wins 99.7% of events, but the average entry is 92¢, so most of those wins were expected and paid a few cents each. The skill is real — the results still beat the prices by 8.56 standard deviations — but it is the thin-margin game described in [buying 99¢ shares](/learn/buying-99-cent-shares), where a single miss costs many wins.

## What does the ranking leave out?

It leaves out three kinds of wallet on purpose. Bots and market makers — wallets that trade hundreds of times a day, hold both sides of a market or earn mostly fee rebates — have their own list of 384 as of 25 November 2026, because their profit is real but is not a view you can follow. One-hit wonders fall away when the best event is removed. Wallets that stop trading slide down.

The bigger omission is everyone else. The wallets were drawn from Polymarket’s leaderboards, so this is a study of winners. It describes what the best records look like, not what a typical trader makes.

## Should you copy a top trader’s bets?

Treat a ranked trader as a source of ideas, not a signal to mirror. You will see their trade after they make it, often at a worse price, and on a thin market your own order can move it further — [why an order fills worse than the card](/learn/why-did-my-order-fill-at-a-worse-price) explains how. A copy grade of easy means none of the usual obstacles apply — five-minute markets, both sides held, near-certain prices, profit from rebates — not that following the trades will pay.

If you do act on one, [size it](/learn/how-to-size-a-position) as your own bet, because if it goes wrong the loss is yours. The [sports list](/traders/sports) and each trader’s profile show what they hold now, with the price they paid next to the price today.
