As of 07:12 UTC on 6 November 2026, the Oddzy market for whether Bitcoin will hit $100,000 by the end of 2026 trades at 10.5¢ — roughly a one-in-ten chance. The same board prices a fall to $70,000 before the year is out at 53.5¢. That gap looks like a bearish call. Most of it is distance: $70,000 is much closer to where Bitcoin trades than $100,000 is.

Every price below is from Oddzy at that timestamp. Each share pays $1 if its condition is met by 11:59 PM ET on 31 December 2026, and nothing if it is not.

## Will Bitcoin hit $100,000 by the end of 2026?

The market says probably not: 10.5¢, on $26,440 of 24-hour volume and $4.05 million traded since it opened. Its window began on 24 November 2025, so the fact that it is still open means Binance BTC/USDT has not printed $100,000 in almost a year.

The distance explains most of the price. Oddzy’s daily market for noon ET on 6 November priced “above $76,000” at 47.1¢ and “above $78,000” at 13.5¢, which puts Bitcoin near $76,000. From there, $100,000 is a 32% rise with eight weeks left. Fortune’s tracker had it at $80,221.40 the morning before. The all-time high, $126,198.07 on 6 October 2025, is priced at 3.25¢ to be beaten this year.

## What does the full year-end ladder price?

Read the ladder as a set of separate touch questions, not as a forecast of where Bitcoin closes. A touch market resolves Yes the instant any one-minute candle reaches the strike — the distinction is laid out in [how a Bitcoin prediction market settles](/learn/bitcoin-prediction-markets).

| Touches by 31 Dec 2026 | YES | 24-hour volume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach $130,000 | 2.05¢ | $70,143 |
| Reach $120,000 | 2.65¢ | $17,770 |
| Reach $110,000 | 4.5¢ | $14,980 |
| [Reach $100,000](/market/will-bitcoin-reach-100000-by-december-31-2026-571-361-361) | 10.5¢ | $26,440 |
| Reach $95,000 | 15.5¢ | $25,347 |
| Reach $90,000 | 22.5¢ | $93,331 |
| Dip to $70,000 | 53.5¢ | $148,025 |
| Dip to $65,000 | 26.5¢ | $9,532 |
| Dip to $60,000 | 17.5¢ | $12,764 |
| Dip to $55,000 | 12.5¢ | $66,623 |
| Dip to $50,000 | 7.5¢ | $59,753 |

Because these are touches, the up and down sides can both resolve Yes. A slide to $70,000 in November followed by a December rally to $90,000 pays both.

## Why is a fall to $70,000 priced above a rise to $90,000?

Mostly because it is closer. $70,000 is about 8% below where the daily markets put Bitcoin on 6 November; $90,000 is about 18% above. A strike that near gets touched often, even in a market going nowhere.

Compare rungs at similar distances and the lean almost disappears:

| Move from about $76,000 | Down rung | Up rung |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roughly 20% | $60,000 at 17.5¢ | $90,000 at 22.5¢ |
| Roughly 25% | $55,000 at 12.5¢ | $95,000 at 15.5¢ |
| Roughly 33% | $50,000 at 7.5¢ | $100,000 at 10.5¢ |

At each distance the up rung is a few cents dearer. So the ladder is not pricing a crash. It is pricing a volatile asset with eight weeks to run, after a month in which Bitcoin fell 7.4% — by Fortune’s figures, from $86,633.41 on 5 October to $80,221.40 on 5 November.

## Why do some long-shot rungs cost more than closer ones?

Because the far rungs are thin, and thin prices are not probabilities. Touching $140,000 means touching $130,000 first, so the $140,000 market can never be worth more. Yet on 6 November it traded at 2.75¢ against 2.05¢ for $130,000. The same happens at $160,000 (3.15¢) over $150,000 (1.3¢), and at $35,000 (2.5¢) over $40,000 (1.85¢). All six share the same 24 November 2025 window.

At the card, YES on $130,000 plus NO on $140,000 costs 99.3¢ and pays at least $1 in every outcome. At the book it usually costs more, because you buy at the offer, not the midpoint — [why orders fill worse than the card](/learn/why-did-my-order-fill-at-a-worse-price). The practical lesson is narrower: below about 3¢, a rung’s price tells you very little.

## What does the Binance hack have to do with how these settle?

Every rung on this ladder settles on one feed: the High or Low of a Binance BTC/USDT one-minute candle. Prices on other exchanges and other pairs do not count.

That made the week of 29 October relevant. Around 4:10 PM ET that day, attackers drained about $6.3 billion from Binance wallets, per TRM Labs and Chainalysis via Al Jazeera, and Binance suspended deposits and withdrawals. Bitcoin fell to a six-week low of $76,368. Neither Al Jazeera nor Kitco reported Binance’s price splitting from other venues. But if a stressed exchange ever prints a one-minute wick nobody else sees, these markets settle on it. That is in the rules, which is why you [read them before the price](/learn/read-the-resolution-rules-first).

## Before you take a position

$10 on the $100,000 rung at 10.5¢ buys about 95 shares, which pay $95 if Bitcoin touches the strike and nothing if it does not. If Bitcoin drifts sideways, the price bleeds toward zero as the deadline nears, so selling early may return far less than you paid. Size it so losing all of it is fine, as in [how to size a position](/learn/how-to-size-a-position). The full ladder is on [the Bitcoin monthly and long-term topic page](/topic/crypto-btc-monthly).
